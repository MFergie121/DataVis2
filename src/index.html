Near, not served

Victoria's hospitality lives next door to public transport. But who gets that closeness isn't decided by wealth — it's decided by the city limits.

Max Fergie · FIT2179 Data Visualisation 2 · May 2026

The setting

Victoria's venues sit remarkably close to a stop

Where the state drinks, dines and gathers — and how short the walk is to public transport. Compare the two side by side, first across all of Victoria, then zoomed in on Melbourne: the venues settle exactly where the network is densest.

The obvious pattern

Where there are venues, there are stops

Of course the pub is near the tram. The co-location shows up by area and by venue type. Each region below is an SA2; within 400 m means a venue is roughly a five-minute walk from its nearest stop.

The expectation

Surely the wealthy suburbs are better served?

It's the intuitive guess: money buys access. Hold that thought.

The reversal

Inside Melbourne, your postcode's wealth barely changes your access

Split the same data by metro and regional and the wealth gradient vanishes. Metro access is flat-and-high across every decile; regional sits well below. The two trend lines are the argument.

The real divide

The fault line is the city limits, not the income line

Geography, head-on: the metro/regional gap, the best- and worst-served places, and how the distance mix shifts by venue type.

The coda

The venues open have the most support for getting home

A closing look at late-opening venues and their proximity to transport. Proximity, not timetable — these stops may not run late.
